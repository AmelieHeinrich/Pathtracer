Material reference
==================

Copy any block below into an `entity` in a .pts file, under its shape and transform. Every key
is optional; anything left out keeps its default, so an opaque grey surface is still just an
albedo and a roughness.

    entity "my thing"
      shape       sphere
      translation 0 1 0
      scale       1 1 1
      <-- paste here

Defaults, for reference:

    albedo              0.7 0.7 0.7
    roughness           1        1 is fully diffuse, 0 is a perfect mirror
    metallic            0        0 is a dielectric, 1 is a conductor
    transmission        0        0 is opaque
    ior                 1.5      only read when transmission > 0
    abbe                0        0 disables dispersion
    absorption          1 1 1    white is no absorption at all
    absorption_distance 1


THE FIVE PARAMETERS
-------------------

roughness       How spread out the reflection is. This is perceptual, not physical: the
                microfacet slope is roughness squared, which is what keeps the interesting
                values spread across the slider instead of crowded into its first tenth.
                Below about 0.03 the surface becomes a true mirror -- see the note on punctual
                lights at the bottom.

metallic        In practice 0 or 1, not a dial. A conductor has no diffuse layer, so its
                albedo stops being a surface colour and becomes the colour of its reflection.
                Values in between are not physical; they exist because they are convenient for
                worn or partly oxidised surfaces.

transmission    How much light goes through instead of bouncing off. Dielectrics only -- a
                metal is opaque, and metallic wins if you set both.

ior             Refractive index, quoted at the sodium d-line (587.6nm) as glass catalogues do.
                Drives both how much the light bends and how strong the reflection is, so a
                higher index gives a more mirror-like surface as well as a more distorting one.

abbe            How much the index varies across the visible band -- the optical measure of
                dispersion. Confusingly, a *lower* number means *more* colour separation.
                0 turns it off entirely and is meaningfully cheaper: a dispersive refraction
                collapses the path to one wavelength, costing roughly 16% more noise.

absorption      Beer-Lambert. Author it as the colour the material becomes after
+ _distance     `absorption_distance` of travel through it. Because it is absorption and not a
                surface tint, it deepens with thickness -- the middle of a sphere goes darker
                and more saturated than its edges, which is exactly what real coloured glass
                does and what a surface colour cannot fake. Distance is in world units, so it
                is relative to how big your object is.


METALS
------
Conductors: metallic 1, and the albedo is the reflectance colour. These are the standard
tabulated linear-sRGB values -- good approximations of the real complex refractive index,
which is what a renderer with measured n,k curves would use instead.

GLASS AND OTHER DIELECTRICS
---------------------------
Set transmission 1 and give it an index. Roughness works here too -- that is frosted glass.

Window glass
      albedo       1 1 1
      roughness    0
      transmission 1
      ior          1.52

Frosted glass       (raise roughness for more diffusion; 0.5 is nearly opal)
      albedo       1 1 1
      roughness    0.25
      transmission 1
      ior          1.52

Water
      albedo       1 1 1
      roughness    0
      transmission 1
      ior          1.333

Ice
      albedo       1 1 1
      roughness    0.12
      transmission 1
      ior          1.31

Acrylic / perspex
      albedo       1 1 1
      roughness    0
      transmission 1

      transmission 1

DISPERSIVE -- gemstones and optical glass
-----------------------------------------
Add an abbe number. Lower means more colour separation. These pairs are close to the real
materials; the index is at the d-line and the Abbe number is the catalogue value.

Crown glass (BK7)   the ordinary optical glass; dispersion present but subtle
      albedo       1 1 1
      roughness    0
      transmission 1
      ior          1.517
      abbe         64

Dense flint (SF11)  about as dispersive as glass gets, and the cheapest way to see fire
      albedo       1 1 1
      roughness    0
      transmission 1
      ior          1.785
      abbe         26

Diamond             high index and strong dispersion together, hence the sparkle
      albedo       1 1 1
      roughness    0
      transmission 1
      ior          2.417
      abbe         55

Sapphire            high index, but genuinely low dispersion
      albedo       1 1 1
      roughness    0
      transmission 1
      ior          1.77
      abbe         72

Cubic zirconia      the diamond simulant; more fire than diamond, less brilliance
      albedo       1 1 1
      roughness    0
      transmission 1
      ior          2.16
      abbe         30


COLOURED GLASS -- absorption
----------------------------
The colour is what the material becomes after `absorption_distance` of travel. Tune the
distance against the size of your object: a distance much larger than the object barely tints
it, much smaller and it goes nearly black.

Green bottle glass
      albedo       1 1 1
      roughness    0
      transmission 1
      ior          1.52
      absorption   0.16 0.62 0.34
      absorption_distance 1

Amber
      albedo       1 1 1
      roughness    0
      transmission 1
      ior          1.55
      absorption   0.85 0.45 0.10
      absorption_distance 1

Red wine
      albedo       1 1 1
      roughness    0
      transmission 1
      ior          1.35
      absorption   0.55 0.06 0.12
      absorption_distance 0.6

Jade                absorbing *and* frosted, which reads as translucent stone
      albedo       1 1 1
      roughness    0.35
      transmission 1
      ior          1.66
      absorption   0.32 0.68 0.44
      absorption_distance 0.4

Emerald
      albedo       1 1 1
      roughness    0
      transmission 1
      ior          1.577
      abbe         70
      absorption   0.22 0.75 0.42
      absorption_distance 1.5

Smoked glass
      albedo       1 1 1
      roughness    0
      transmission 1
      ior          1.52
      absorption   0.35 0.35 0.38
      absorption_distance 1

THINGS WORTH KNOWING
--------------------

Roughness 0 and punctual lights.
    A perfectly smooth surface is a delta: it reflects exactly one direction, so a point,
    spot, or directional light has zero chance of lying on it and contributes nothing. This is
    correct, not a bug -- a real mirror does not have a highlight, it has a reflection. If you
    want a visible highlight from a lamp, give the surface some roughness (0.05 is plenty), or
    use an `area` light, which has real extent.

Solid glass lit from behind by a punctual light comes out about 15% dim.
    Direct lighting through a refractive surface assumes the light sits where it appears in a
    straight line. That holds across a *single* interface -- a pane, a window -- and is exact
    there. Through anything solid the light has to bend twice, so the assumption breaks and the
    direct contribution is deliberately dropped rather than trusted; the light still arrives,
    by the paths that refract properly. Use an area light or emissive geometry behind glass and
    the result is correct with no caveat.

Do not nest transmissive objects.
    Absorption tracks one medium at a time, so a gem inside water will get the boundaries
    wrong -- leaving the inner object clears the outer one's absorption.

Dispersion is noisier.
    Setting `abbe` collapses each path to a single wavelength, which costs roughly 16% more
    noise on that object. Leave it at 0 unless you want the colour separation.

Very rough metals lose a little energy.
    A single-scattering microfacet model does not put back the light that would have bounced a
    second time across the microsurface. At roughness 1 that is about 3%, and it falls off
    quickly as the surface smooths.

Absorption needs transmission.
    It describes what happens to light *inside* the material, so it does nothing on an opaque
    surface. Likewise `ior` and `abbe` are only read when transmission is above zero.
